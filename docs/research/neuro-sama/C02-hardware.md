# C02-hardware — Inference hardware & hosting
Verdict counts: {'confirmed': 7, 'disputed': 4, 'refuted': 1, 'likely': 6, 'unverifiable': 3}

## Summary A (evidence hunter)
Neuro's inference hardware is now the best-documented part of her backend, and the tier is higher than the finders believed: the CPU and RAM are not aggregator lore but Vedal's own on-stream words (18 May 2026 — "128 [GB]… DDR5 ECC" and "24 cores… Thread Ripper Pro… Zen 5… 48 [threads]… the 9965WX"), and the 25 May 2026 build stream adds an ASUS Pro WS "Sage"-class board chosen for PCIe lanes "because we're going to put a lot of GPUs in here", dual 1200 W PSUs (one redundant) feeding a UPS, a wheeled ~50 kg rack, and at least one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition Max-Q (96 GB GDDR7, 300 W) which Vedal tells Neuro she must share with Evil. The official Neuro-sama blog of 25 May 2026 independently supplies 28 TB of storage, "dual-UPS", ProxMox as hypervisor, and the GPU's ~$10,000 price — so storage, hypervisor and price are T2, while CPU, RAM, motherboard class, PSU/UPS topology and GPU model are T1. Self-hosting is therefore settled: Neuro and Evil run on hardware Vedal owns and assembled by hand, no cloud provider has ever been named, and the older clone-blog speculation that "he is in fact running at least some of the AI in the cloud, presumably with a provider" is refuted clone-derived guesswork from January 2024. What remains genuinely unknown is deployment: the blog shows the machine still being built on 25 May 2026, the only evidence that it was "working fine but Neuro isn't on it yet" around 14 June 2026 is an unread YouTube clip known solely through a search-engine summary (T5), and four post-build weekly blog posts (1 Jun, 15 Jun, 24 Aug, 31 Aug 2026, all fetched in full) contain zero infrastructure content — so whether live inference was ever cut over to the Blackwell server is formally unresolved as of 22 September 2026. Also unknown: the number of GPUs actually installed (Vedal says more were planned but "we can't install today"), the fine-tuning method (the "likely LoRA" line traces to an undated, unlinked community note), any spec list for the pre-2026 production machine, and the total build cost — the "$30,000" figure exists only inside a reaction-video title. Two hardware-adjacent facts are firm and useful: Twitch-event ingestion shared the AI machine badly enough that the January 2025 subathon drove CPU usage to 100 % (Sportskeeda, not Wikipedia, which carries no hardware content at all), and the lava-lamp / plasma-globe stream props were literally wired to the host machine's RAM usage as a crash warning before being retired after a hardware upgrade. Finally, the binding constraint before May 2026 was software, not silicon: Vedal attributes years of stutter and low FPS to Unity memory leaks that the Apr 2026 rewrite eliminated, after which a VR stream ran above 100 FPS with no leak.

## Summary B (skeptic)
Neuro-sama's inference hardware is, uniquely among this project's topics, genuinely well-established at the top tier for one moment in time: on 18 and 25 May 2026 Vedal announced and then hand-built on camera a server for Neuro and Evil comprising a 24-core/48-thread Zen 5 Threadripper PRO (SKU heard as 9965WX), 128 GB DDR5 ECC RAM on an 8-slot ASUS "Sage"-class workstation board chosen for PCIe lanes, dual 1200 W PSUs (one redundant, staged for GPUs not yet installed) feeding a UPS in a wheeled rack, and at least one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition Max-Q; the official blog independently adds 28 TB of storage, dual-UPS, ProxMox as hypervisor and the GPU's ~$10,000 price (T1/T2, verified by direct fetch and by verbatim stream transcript). That single GPU is the hard architectural constraint worth putting in the report: an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell carries 96 GB of GDDR7, and Vedal states on stream that Neuro and Evil share it, which bounds whatever LLM, TTS and vision models are co-resident. What is NOT established is deployment: the blog describes the machine as unfinished on 25 May 2026, the only claimed status checkpoint after that ("not on the new server yet, but working fine", ~14 June 2026) exists solely as a search-engine paraphrase of an unreadable video title, and every weekly blog post from June through 21 September 2026 — including the September posts the earlier finder wrongly reported as non-existent — is pure in-character narrative with zero infrastructure content, so cutover is formally unresolved as of this research date. Everything predating May 2026 is far weaker: "self-hosted on Vedal's own GPUs" before 2026 rests on an undated secondhand stream relay, no CPU/RAM/storage list has ever been published for whatever machine ran her in 2023–2025, and the November 2024 IRL build stream is date-confirmed by an archived Twitch VOD but its RTX 4090 was (per wiki only) defective and refunded and was never tied to production inference. Three items in this cluster should be struck rather than softened: the January 2025 "CPU hit 100%" incident, whose Wikipedia citation is false and which no other source carries; the 2021 GPT-2/cloud-provider story, which is clone-author speculation contradicted by the 2026 evidence; and the Grokipedia i9-10900K/RTX 4090/128 GB list, which is T6 AI-generated content with no source. The "~2B parameters / q2_k" lore and "LoRA fine-tuning" remain unverifiable community inference, and the q2_k label in particular is more plausibly an artifact of hobbyist llama.cpp clone culture than a degraded real disclosure. Total build cost (~$30,000) and Vedal's alleged cost/Twitch-revenue remarks are title-only and aggregator-only respectively and should not be quoted as figures.
