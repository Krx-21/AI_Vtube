# C11-safety — Filter, safety, incidents
Verdict counts: {'disputed': 8, 'likely': 5, 'confirmed': 2, 'meta': 1}

## Summary A (evidence hunter)
What is solidly established at T1 (Vedal's own words in named outlets) is narrow: he told Vice on 4 January 2023 that "there are of course many filters in place to prevent her being inappropriate or offensive – including some powered by AI, but, there currently needs to be a human there to moderate," and that a team monitors both Neuro and chat; and he confirmed the ban himself in a Discord message quoted by Kotaku on 11 January 2023 ("banned for 2 weeks obviously, not sure why something about hateful conduct"). The January 2023 incident is the best-documented item in this cluster: 28 December 2022 Holocaust answer ("I'm not sure if I believe it"), two-week ban from 11 January 2023 under Twitch's hateful-conduct policy, return on 25 January 2023 — corroborated by Kotaku, Anime News Network, Dexerto and Automaton West, though Twitch itself never confirmed the specific trigger. The distinctive UX claim — that a blocked output is visibly replaced with the word "filtered" rather than silently suppressed — sits at T4, cited by Wikipedia to the peer-reviewed Wu & Lingel 2025 study, not to Kotaku as the finder assumed, and corroborated independently by NamuWiki, which reports the literal on-screen string "Filtered." plus a two-tier design (keyword filter + an additional LLM-based filter) and a control-panel force-mute that Vedal has reportedly not needed since the early-2023 filter overhaul. The three post-ban changes (filter hardening, manual training-data curation, Vedal moderating chat) are only T4: Kotaku's published text attests the filter/response work, and Vice shows a moderation team already existed a week before the ban, so "started moderating" means increased personal involvement, not the introduction of moderation. Nothing in the official VedalAI neuro-sdk specification addresses filtering, moderation or safety at all — validation is explicitly the game developer's responsibility — so there is no T2 evidence for any filter architecture. The prompt-priority "Injection object" design must be dropped as clone contamination (kimjammer/Neuro), and the only concrete prompt-injection defence described anywhere in the claim set therefore has no basis in Neuro's real backend. What remains unknown: the filter's actual matching mechanism and word list, whether any of this survived the April 2026 rewrite (no 2025–2026 source describes the filter at all), how many chat messages are ingested per turn or with what priority, and whether any chat-driven jailbreak has ever succeeded — only self-initiated bypasses (spelling out letters, symbol substitution, homophones) are documented, and only at T5.

## Summary B (skeptic)
What is solidly established for this cluster is the January 2023 incident chain: Vedal's own words, quoted by Kotaku on 11 January 2023 ("Okay so banned for 2 weeks obviously, not sure why something about hateful conduct"), confirm the two-week suspension and its stated reason at T1, and ANN/Dexerto/Wikipedia independently tie it to a 28 December 2022 stream reply about the Holocaust at T3. The single best primary description of the safety stack is still Vedal's 4 January 2023 quote to Vice — "many filters in place... including some powered by AI, but, there currently needs to be a human there to moderate" — which is T1 but is a pre-ban, January-2023 snapshot; no source at any tier describes the filter after the March–June 2026 rewrite, so the report must not present it as current. The visible-"filtered" output behaviour is the best-supported design detail below T1: Wikipedia cites the peer-reviewed Wu & Lingel (2025) study in New Media & Society, and NamuWiki (KR and EN) independently records the literal rendered string plus a two-tier keyword-filter-plus-LLM-classifier description and a manual control-panel kill switch. A significant correction this pass: the widely-repeated "post-ban remediation" narrative is a dating error — Wikipedia's own sentence reads "Prior to the ban, Vedal said in an interview with Kotaku that he improved her filter... began manually curating her training data... and started moderating her Twitch chat", so those three changes were described as already done before 11 January, and only "improved the chat filters and Neuro-sama's responses" is actually in Kotaku's text (the training-data-curation detail exists only in Wikipedia's paraphrase). The "Do you think I'll ever be real?" moment is not a separate undated 2025 event: it and "I want to be real Vedal" are from the same 23 December 2025 stream in the same Dexerto article, and the "hallucination within her persona card" gloss is uncited commentary. What remains unknown: the filter's actual implementation and blocklist, whether any of it survived the 2026 rewrite, how many chat messages are ingested or how subscribers are prioritised (no figure exists anywhere in public — do not guess), and whether any successful chat-driven prompt injection has ever been documented; the only concrete prompt-injection-defence architecture in the whole claim set is kimjammer/Neuro's "Injection" priority object, verified this pass as belonging to a self-described 7-day clone and therefore inadmissible as evidence about Vedal's backend.
